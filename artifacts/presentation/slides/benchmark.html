This is how we know the method works, and where it stops working. We take ten real unit processes, aggregate them the same way BAFU does, and throw the input list away — then give the algorithm a candidate input list of deliberately varying quality and see how much of the truth it gets back. The first two rows are what an S1 report feels like, and the algorithm is reliable there. The third row is a report that omits its minor lines. The fourth is the dangerous one, and the reason we never let S4 stand alone: when we pad the input list with plausible-looking wrong processes, the optimiser still reproduces 82 percent of the aggregated flows, while giving a material amount to four inputs that were never in the process. Matching the aggregated flows is necessary but not sufficient. That is the single most important thing we learned.
