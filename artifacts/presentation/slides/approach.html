Step two · the method

Read the report, then pick a route

What the evidence gives us

S1  Its own inventory, one number per input. Copy it.

S2  No inventory, but a unit process of the same product exists. Reuse it.

S3  Names the inputs, not every amount. Fit what is missing.

S4  Only the aggregated flows. Benchmark only.

Where amounts are still missing, we solve for them

A report often names an input without a quantity, or gives a range. But we do know the aggregated flows the unit process has to reproduce.

Known: the aggregated flows  ·  Known: the input list
Unknown: the amounts  →  fit them

Each candidate input carries its own known inventory, so this is a least‑squares fit over ~1,800 elementary flows at once — no impact score, no weighting of climate against toxicity.

The pipeline is the same for every dataset: find all system processes, pull their metadata and their cited PDF, read what the report actually contains, decide which of four routes applies, and only then fit whatever amounts are still missing. The routes, as they turned out in the data. S1: the report prints this dataset's own inventory with one number per input, and we copy it; that is rare, burnt shale is our clean case. S2: there is no inventory, but a unit process of the same product already exists, at another site, in another grade, as an older version, and once as BAFU's own dis-aggregated twin; we reuse its structure and refit the amounts. S3 is most of the work: something names the inputs, whether the dataset's own process description, a partial table, printed ranges or published comparison data, but not every amount. The main feedstock then comes from the reaction equation, the utilities from BAFU's generic chemicals module, and the fit sets what is left. S4, fitting with no evidence at all, we only ran in the benchmark. And every rebuild also carries S5, the residual block. The optimisation is the part worth explaining slowly: we are not inventing data out of nothing. We know the answer the unit process has to produce, because the system process gives us the aggregated flows. We know, from the report, which inputs went in. What we solve for is the amounts. The last point on the right matters for credibility: we never compare impact scores, we compare all eighteen hundred elementary flows, so we cannot accidentally trade climate performance against something else.
