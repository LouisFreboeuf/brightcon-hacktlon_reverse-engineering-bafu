Two-day hackathon project. Walk the picture left to right. On the left, a system process: where the inputs should be there is nothing, and instead about eighteen hundred elementary flows come straight out of the product. On the right, the same product as a unit process: four or five named inputs, each of which is itself a process with its own upstream, and only the handful of emissions that actually happen at this plant. System processes exist for good reasons, mostly confidentiality. They are a problem because you cannot see which inputs drive the result, you cannot change an assumption, and you cannot trace an impact back to its origin. Our goal was to find every system process in BAFU, work out which of them can be dis-aggregated back into unit processes, and be honest about where that is not possible.
