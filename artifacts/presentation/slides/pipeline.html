Before the details, the whole path in one picture. Step one, find: every dataset BAFU flags as a system process, plus the ones that give themselves away by having no production input at all; that gave us 138, a lower bound. Step two, read: we locate the report the dataset cites and extract what it says about inputs and amounts, or, where no report helps, we look for a unit process of the same product elsewhere in BAFU. What that evidence gives decides the route, and the three coloured lanes show how each route runs through the same steps. S1, one dataset: the report prints its full inventory, we copy every amount, and there is nothing left to fit. S2, eleven datasets: a unit process of the same product exists, we reuse its structure and refit its amounts. S3, thirty-nine datasets: the evidence names the inputs but not every amount, we build the list from the evidence and the chemistry and fit whatever is open. The example slide that follows shows what step two can find in a report; three more are in the backup at the end. Step four, fit: the small picture is the whole idea, the known inventories of the inputs times the unknown input amounts should equal the known aggregated elementary flows, all eighteen hundred at once. Step five, build and check: the unit process is written into BAFU with its inputs linked to real datasets, and for every route a residual block, S5, covers whatever the rebuild does not explain, so the hybrid still reproduces the original exactly. Then we compare every elementary flow with the original and export it. The route counts are the ones on the results slide.
