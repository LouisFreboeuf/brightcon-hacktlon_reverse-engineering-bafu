Where it stops

Fitted amounts fill gaps; they are not estimates. On the real datasets, 70 % of those with a range end on its edge.

Background drift is untested. The benchmark shares its background with its targets; the real originals were built on older data.

No meaningful match score yet. Counting flows within ±10 % is dominated by background trace flows: BAFU's own dis-aggregated epoxy scores 0.4 %.

Labels need reading, not counting. Whether a table is this dataset's own inventory is said in the prose; the automatic labels need a manual pass.

Five things we can stand behind. We found 138 system processes in BAFU, not the 101 its export flags; the other 37 give themselves away by having no production input. That is a lower bound: a partly aggregated dataset would pass our test unseen. We rebuilt 51 of them as explicit unit processes that link into BAFU, and exported them so anyone can import them into their own Brightway project. None of this changes a result, because every rebuild carries the residual block that reproduces the original exactly. On synthetic cases the method recovers the amounts when it is given the right input list, and a padded list hurts more than a short one. And the real bottleneck is evidence: only 12 of the 138 reports have a readable inventory table at all. Then be plain about the limits. On the real datasets the fitted amounts are gap-fillers, not estimates: seventy percent of those with a range end on its edge, which means the data cannot pin them down. Our benchmark never tested background drift, and the real originals were computed on older background data than the one we link to, which is the most likely reason. We do not yet have a meaningful score for how well a rebuild matches its original; counting flows is dominated by background trace flows, and BAFU's own dis-aggregated epoxy scores 0.4 percent on that count. And the classification has to be read, not counted: whether a table is the dataset's own inventory is stated in a sentence, not in the numbers. Next steps, if asked: test the fit on epoxy, the one real dataset with a known answer; rerun the benchmark with an older background; and reclassify every dataset by reading, with the deciding sentence recorded in its spec.
