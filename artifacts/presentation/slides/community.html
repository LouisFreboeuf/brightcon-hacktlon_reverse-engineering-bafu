Three things work today. First, an end-to-end pipeline: give it an aggregated system process and whatever extra information exists, an input table in the cited report, a free-text process description, or a unit process of the same product elsewhere in the database, and it produces a unit process whose inputs link into the database, with every amount pointing to the sentence it came from and a residual block so that no result changes. Second, we applied it to BAFU-2026: 51 of the 138 system processes we investigated are rebuilt and exported, with an importer that writes them into any Brightway project that holds BAFU-2026. Third, we benchmarked it on a hundred unit processes that we aggregated ourselves, so the right answer is known. Given the right input list, 787 of 803 amounts come back within twenty percent, and a few wrong candidates on the list are set to zero; with no list at all, the fit matches ninety-eight percent of the flows with a process of its own making, so a good flow match does not prove the inputs. The reading step on its own, run end to end on a hundred reports, found the right pages for 58 and extracted input lists with 56 percent recall and 71 percent precision. Next steps. Quality needs domain experts: without a known answer we can only compare elementary flows, and on the real datasets they match loosely. Twenty-three percent of the flows both sides have are within ten percent, and fossil CO2 is within ten percent for 12 of the 51. Likely reasons: the originals were computed on older background data, and even BAFU's own dis-aggregated epoxy misses its aggregate's fossil CO2 by a factor of 2.7; amounts the reports name but never give, which the fit pushes to a bound, as eight in ten of the amounts with printed bounds are; and modelling choices such as allocation or the compartment an emission is booked to. Where there is little or no information, the algorithm has nothing to work from, and that is work for people with system knowledge. The best outcome would be peer-reviewed, transparent models that rebuild a whole class of similar products at once, one polymerisation model for the plastics eco-profiles, one composition model for the cement blends, rather than one dataset at a time. And we can say where to start: ranked by how much of the rest of BAFU depends on them, 19 of the 138 carry eighty percent, while the 53 without any report carry less than one percent.
